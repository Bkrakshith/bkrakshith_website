Fragrance Castle: humanoid robots in a perfume business

A Kyne Robotics case study for Fragrance Castle, a perfume business. Two AgiBot G2 humanoids and an autonomous mobile robot restock a warehouse, pack online orders and refill a luxury boutique. The project covered the site design, the build of the assets and robots, and a 120 s film rendered in Isaac Sim.

What

Fragrance Castle sells perfume online and in a boutique. The film shows a full working site for that business: a 50 × 30 m warehouse hall with eight rack aisles and a 10 m clear height, a packing line, a mezzanine office with a glass view of the floor, and a covered linkway to a 20 × 15 m luxury boutique.

Three robots do the work. G2-H is an AgiBot G2 with five-finger hands. It moves and opens master cartons, packs orders and restocks the boutique. G2-S is an AgiBot G2 with a suction tool. It unloads perfume boxes, shelves them and picks orders. An AMR carries a tray trolley between the racks, the packing line and the boutique.

How

The project ran as a systems engineering V-model. Stakeholder needs became requirements. The requirements became a site layout and a list of robot subtasks. Five feasibility studies tested the risky parts before the build. Each phase passed a gate, checked from evidence, before the next phase started.

The build was split into four parallel work packages: the building shell and office, the warehouse and packing line, the boutique, and the robots with their props. One OpenUSD stage assembles all of them.

Results

The film is complete: 120 s, 16 shots and 2,880 frames at 1080p. Every shot passed the collision and motion gate before it was rendered. The delivery also included stills, a title card and LinkedIn banners. All work is in simulation. No real robot did these tasks.

System architecture

Select the diagram to enlarge it

One layout file

One Python file sets the position and size of every zone, rack, door and station. The drawings, the USD scene and the tests all read it, so a moved rack is a one-line change that every check sees.

One world state

The story state passes from shot to shot: robot poses, joint values, and the position of every box, carton and tester. A box that G2-S shelves in shot 7 is on that shelf in shot 9.

One hard gate

A shot renders only when the validator passes every frame and the bake hash matches. A shot that cannot pass is simplified or removed. It is never rendered with a defect.

The site

The site follows Singapore building rules. The hall has eight exits and the boutique has three. The longest travel distance to an exit is 17.4 m in the hall, against a two-way limit of 40 m, and 16.4 m in the boutique, against a one-way limit of 16.7 m. The mezzanine is larger than 60 m², so it has two stairs. The boutique entrance has a 1:12 ramp.

The warehouse floor is for robots only during operations. People use a walkway with barriers to the office stairs. The plan is design intent only. A Qualified Person has not reviewed it.

The story

The film follows three scenarios. Inbound: G2-H lifts a master carton off a pallet and opens it, G2-S unloads the 12 perfumes and shelves them, and G2-H flattens the empty carton into the recycling cage. Online order: G2-S picks the order into a trolley tray, the AMR takes it to the packing line, and G2-H packs it into a shipping box that goes through the tape sealer. Boutique: the AMR and G2-H go through the linkway, and G2-H restocks the display.

Robots within real limits

Every lift stays inside the G2 specification: 10 kg with two arms and 5 kg with one. A master carton is 32 × 32 × 42 cm and holds 12 perfumes, about 8 kg. An earlier carton size held 18 perfumes and broke the limit, so it was changed. Reach was measured on the simulated G2 before the shelf heights were frozen: 0.30 to 1.60 m in the warehouse and 0.40 to 1.45 m in the boutique. The first trolley design had three levels that the robot could not serve from above, so the trolley now has one tray deck at 0.80 m.

G2-H uses two Shadow Hand E3M5 hands from MuJoCo Menagerie, on wrist adapters. Each grasp is solved for its prop, with all fingertip contacts at 0.71 to 0.80 mm and no penetration. G2-S has a suction tool with a bellows cup and a valve block. Its attachment holds with a drift below 10−9 m.

Motion and checks

The first rough cut passed the automatic checks, but a review found four defects: arms that passed through each other, a board pushed through the mesh of the recycling cage, a wrist flip, and time jumps in one camera angle. The validator could not see thin parts such as cardboard and wire mesh.

The validator was then rebuilt. Regression tests now reproduce the four defects and must fail. An exact crossing test checks thin geometry. The validator also fails a shot for an IK error above 10 mm, a joint flip, or a cut that splices two times of the same camera angle. All motion follows a slow profile: at most 40% of joint speed, 0.25 m/s in transit and 0.05 m/s for the last 6 cm. Every placement goes up over the rim, aligns, descends vertically, rests and retreats.

Rendering

Isaac Sim renders each shot with RTX real-time path tracing at 32 subframes, in 2.6 to 3.3 s per frame. The boutique shots take 6.6 s per frame, because glass, coloured liquid and brass need 12 light bounces to look correct. The full film rendered overnight on one RTX 3070. A watchdog on the render jobs stops a job that hangs on the GPU.
