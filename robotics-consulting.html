Who it is for

Robotics programmes fail more often at the edges than at the core. The robot works in a demo, then the pilot stalls: the use case was too broad, the integration was underestimated, or nobody owned the path from pilot to production. Rakshith works with teams that want to avoid this.

  • Startups building a robot product, or adding robotics to an existing product, that need architecture, a first prototype or a technical lead.
  • Businesses and manufacturers that want to start a robotics or automation programme and need a clear first use case, a feasibility study and a pilot plan.
  • Universities and research groups that want to set up a robotics laboratory or programme with industry partners.

How a robotics programme starts

Five phases
01 · Discover

Choose the first use case

  • Map the workflow on site and list the candidate tasks.
  • Select one task with a measurable end state and a realistic chance of success. The hardest task is rarely the right first task.
02 · Architect

Design the system

  • Define the requirements, acceptance criteria and safety concept.
  • Choose the platform, the sensors and the software stack. Decide what to buy and what to build.
03 · Simulate

Prove it in simulation first

  • Build a digital twin of the cell or the site. Test layouts, controllers and navigation before the hardware arrives.
  • Identify the real actuators and sensors, so the simulation matches the hardware.
04 · Pilot

Run one instrumented pilot

  • Run the pilot next to normal operations. Record logs, video and operator feedback from the first week.
  • Measure the result against the acceptance criteria from phase 02.
05 · Scale

Move from pilot to production

  • Plan support, spare parts, training and the next use cases.
  • Build the internal team, so the programme does not depend on one person.
“Identify the system before you trust it.”

Why a hardware and software generalist

Most robotics problems sit between disciplines. A controller can work in simulation and fail on a real actuator. A good mechanical design can be impossible to manufacture at the target cost. A specialist sees one side of the problem; a generalist sees the system.

Rakshith works across mechanical design, integration, control, simulation, machine learning and product delivery. This makes Rakshith a high-velocity robotics generalist: one person who can take a robotics idea through architecture, prototype and pilot, and who knows when to bring in a specialist.
